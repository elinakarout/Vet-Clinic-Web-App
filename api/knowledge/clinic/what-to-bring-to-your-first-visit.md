# What to bring to your first visit

Please arrive about 10 minutes early so we have time to register you and your
pet properly.

## Paperwork

- **Previous medical records**, if your pet has been seen anywhere else. Ask
  your old practice to send them across, or bring printouts.
- **The vaccination card or certificate**, so we can see exactly what has been
  given and when. Without it we may have to restart a vaccination course.
- **Microchip number and registration details**, if your pet is already chipped.
- **Pet insurance details** — the insurer, the policy number, and a claim form
  if you already have one.
- **Adoption or breeder paperwork** for a new puppy, kitten or rescue.
- Any **pet passport or travel documentation** if the visit is about travel.

## Medication and diet

- **Everything your pet is currently taking**, in the original packaging —
  prescription medicines, flea and worm treatments, supplements. Bring the boxes
  rather than a list; the dose and strength matter.
- The name of the **food** your pet eats, and roughly how much per day.

## Samples, if we have asked for one

- **Urine:** collect a fresh sample the same morning in a clean container.
- **Faeces:** a fresh sample from the last 24 hours, in a sealed bag or pot.
- If your pet has been vomiting, a photo is genuinely useful.

## Getting your pet here safely

- **Cats and small animals** must travel in a secure carrier — not in your arms
  and not loose in the car. Put a familiar blanket inside. Covering the carrier
  with a towel makes most cats much calmer in the waiting room.
- **Dogs** must be on a lead, or in a carrier if small. Bring a muzzle if your
  dog has ever been anxious or snappy at the vet; it is not a judgement, it just
  keeps everyone safe.
- **Rabbits and rodents** travel best in a solid carrier with hay and a hiding
  place. Bring a bonded companion along for company.
- Do not feed your pet in the hour before travelling if they get car sick.

## Useful things to think about beforehand

We will ask, so it helps to have thought about it:

- What is the problem, and when did it start?
- Has it got better, worse, or stayed the same?
- Any change in appetite, drinking, weight, energy, or toilet habits?
- Any coughing, sneezing, vomiting, diarrhoea or limping?
- Is your pet insured, and is the policy lifetime cover?

Writing down two or three questions before you come is a good idea. It is easy
to forget them once the consultation starts.

## Fasting

Only fast your pet if we have specifically told you to — normally before surgery
or certain blood tests. For a routine first consultation, feed as usual, and
**bring some high-value treats** so we can make the visit a positive one.

## Payment

Payment is due at the end of the visit, by cash or card. Most insurers require
you to pay us and then claim the money back yourself, so check what your policy
says before you come.
