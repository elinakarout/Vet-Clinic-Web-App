# Vaccination schedules

This page is a general guide to what is due and when. Your veterinarian will
confirm the right schedule for your individual pet at the appointment — an
animal with an unknown history, a health condition, or a course that was
interrupted may need a different plan.

Vaccination appointments include a full health check, so book a standard
consultation slot.

## Dogs

### Puppies

| Age | Vaccination |
|---|---|
| 6–8 weeks | First DHPP (distemper, hepatitis, parvovirus, parainfluenza) |
| 10–12 weeks | Second DHPP |
| 14–16 weeks | Third DHPP, plus rabies |
| 12 months | First annual booster |

A puppy is not fully protected until **two weeks after the final dose** of the
primary course. Until then, keep them away from unvaccinated dogs and from
places where lots of dogs go.

### Adult dogs

| Vaccination | How often |
|---|---|
| DHPP booster | Every 12 months |
| Rabies | Every 12 months, or as local regulations require |
| Kennel cough (Bordetella) | Every 12 months; required by most boarding kennels |
| Leptospirosis | Every 12 months where risk is high |

If a booster is more than three months overdue, the primary course may need to
be restarted rather than simply topped up. Bring your dog in and we will advise.

## Cats

### Kittens

| Age | Vaccination |
|---|---|
| 8–9 weeks | First FVRCP (rhinotracheitis, calicivirus, panleukopenia) |
| 12 weeks | Second FVRCP, plus feline leukaemia (FeLV) if going outdoors |
| 14–16 weeks | Rabies |
| 12 months | First annual booster |

### Adult cats

| Vaccination | How often |
|---|---|
| FVRCP booster | Every 12 months |
| Rabies | Every 12 months, or as local regulations require |
| Feline leukaemia (FeLV) | Every 12 months for cats with outdoor access |

Indoor-only cats still need core vaccination. Infections travel indoors on
clothing and shoes, and an indoor cat that escapes is completely unprotected.

## Rabbits

| Vaccination | How often |
|---|---|
| Myxomatosis and rabbit haemorrhagic disease (RHD1 and RHD2) | Every 12 months |

Rabbits can be vaccinated from **5 weeks** of age. Both diseases are usually
fatal and myxomatosis is spread by biting insects, so indoor rabbits need
protecting too.

## After a vaccination

Mild tiredness, a slightly reduced appetite, or a small tender lump at the
injection site for a day or two is normal.

Contact us straight away if you see facial swelling, hives, repeated vomiting,
difficulty breathing or collapse. These are signs of an allergic reaction and
need treating urgently — see *Emergency guidance*.
