# Post-surgery care

Your pet has had a general anaesthetic. Follow the discharge instructions your
veterinarian gave you — the notes below are the general guidance that applies to
most procedures, and the specific advice for your pet takes priority.

## The first 24 hours

Your pet will be drowsy, unsteady, and may not behave normally. That is the
anaesthetic wearing off and it is expected.

- Keep them somewhere warm, quiet and confined, away from other pets and from
  children.
- Do not let them use stairs, jump onto furniture, or climb.
- Offer a small amount of water once you get home. Offer a small, bland meal
  that evening — around a quarter of the usual portion. A skipped meal on the
  first evening is not a worry.
- Some pets are whiny or restless for the first night. Keep them company and
  keep the room dark and quiet.

Cats must be kept **indoors** with a litter tray for the whole recovery period,
not just the first night.

## The wound

Check the wound twice a day. It should look clean and dry, and the edges should
be together.

Normal in the first few days:

- Slight redness or bruising around the incision
- A small amount of clear or slightly blood-tinged fluid on the first day
- A small firm lump under the skin as it heals

Call us if you see:

- Bleeding, or fluid still leaking after the first day
- Yellow or green discharge, or a bad smell
- Swelling that is getting worse rather than better
- Gaping edges, or the wound opening
- Heat, or obvious pain when the area is near-touched

## Stopping them licking

Licking is the single most common reason a wound breaks down. Your pet must wear
the collar or body suit we sent home **at all times**, including overnight, and
including when you are watching them — it takes seconds to pull a stitch out.

If the collar is causing real distress, call us and we will find an alternative
rather than have you remove it.

## Exercise

- **Dogs:** short lead walks only for toilet purposes. No running, no off-lead
  exercise, no ball games, no rough play with other dogs, for the period your
  vet specified — usually 10 to 14 days.
- **Cats:** indoors only for the whole recovery period.
- Do not bath your pet or let the wound get wet until the wound has fully healed
  and any stitches are out.

## Medication

Give every dose of the medication we sent home, at the times on the label, and
finish the course even if your pet seems completely better.

**Never give human painkillers.** Paracetamol and ibuprofen are poisonous to
dogs and cats, and paracetamol kills cats very quickly. If you think the pain
relief is not working, call us for a different prescription rather than adding
anything yourself.

## Follow-up

Most patients need a post-operative check **3 days** after surgery, and stitch
removal at **10 to 14 days** if the stitches are not dissolvable. Book the
follow-up before you leave, or through your account.

A follow-up consultation about the same problem within 14 days is charged at the
reduced follow-up rate. Post-operative checks that are part of the surgery are
included — we will tell you which applies.

## Call us straight away if

- Your pet has not eaten anything 24 hours after surgery
- There is repeated vomiting, or diarrhoea with blood
- Your pet is not urinating or passing faeces after 48 hours
- Breathing is fast, laboured or noisy
- The gums are pale, white or blue
- Your pet is still very drowsy, or cannot stand, the day after surgery
- Your pet collapses, has a seizure, or cries out in pain

These are emergencies. See *Emergency guidance* — come straight in, or go to a
24-hour emergency hospital if we are closed.
